How to write a one-page product brief

Demo page for PageVera · 6 min read

Why one page

A product brief exists to get a team to agree on what problem is worth solving before anyone designs a solution. Long documents hide that decision under detail. A single page forces you to say who has the problem, how you know, and what result would tell you the work paid off.

Keep the brief separate from the spec. The brief is for deciding whether to build; the spec is for deciding how. Mixing them invites a debate about button colors when the real question is whether the feature should exist.

The five sections

SectionThe question it answers
ProblemWho is stuck, and what does it cost them today?
EvidenceWhat did we see in interviews, tickets or data?
ProposalWhat are we betting on, in one paragraph?
Success metricWhich number moves if this works, and by how much?
Open questionsWhat would change our mind?

Choosing a success metric

Pick one number the team can watch weekly. If the honest answer is that nothing measurable will change, the brief has done its job by surfacing that early.

Getting it reviewed

Send the brief a day ahead and ask each reviewer for one risk they see. Collect the answers under open questions. When the meeting starts, the discussion is already about the hard parts.

If a reviewer can't restate the problem after reading the brief, rewrite the problem section first.
